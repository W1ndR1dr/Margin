# Validating Margin's AI segmentation against HaN-Seg expert contours

Generated by `tools/validation/hanseg_eval.py`. Every number in the tables is produced
by that script from `results.json` / `summary.json` (`--tables`) and is never retyped.

> **Bottom line.** Large, well-circumscribed organs (mandible, parotid, submandibular
> gland, thyroid, globe) are good enough to hand a surgeon as a starting contour. The
> carotid is not, and the reason is structural rather than cosmetic. Nothing in the
> laryngeal framework, nodal levels, or IJV is validated by this run at all.

---

## 1. What was run

| | |
|---|---|
| Dataset | HaN-Seg v1.0 (Zenodo 7442914), 42 cases, 30 expert OAR contours per case |
| Cases scored | `case_01`, `case_05`, `case_12` (**N = 3**, see §6) |
| Backend | `127.0.0.1:8765`, AI API v0.3, TotalSegmentator 2.18.0, 12 threads, CPU only |
| Ground truth | the original `*.seg.nrrd` organ files, read in place from `%LOCALAPPDATA%\HNRad\datasets\HaN-Seg\raw` |
| Predictions | `GET /api/analysis/label/{id}/mask`, gzip `uint8` `(z,y,x)` |
| Image size | 1024 x 1024 x 134-209, 0.5576-0.6826 mm in plane, 2.0 mm slices |

Jobs per case, each a separate `POST /api/ai/segment` with `fast: false`:

| # | model | task | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| a | `totalseg` | `headneck_bones_vessels` | internal carotid, IJV, laryngeal cartilages |
| b | `totalseg` | `head_glands_cavities` | parotid, submandibular, globe, optic nerve |
| c | `totalseg` | `craniofacial_structures` | **added to the brief** - the only offered task containing `mandible`, which the report needs |
| d | `totalseg` | `total`, `roi_subset` = spinal_cord, esophagus, thyroid_gland, brain, vertebrae_C1-C7 | common carotid, cord, oesophagus, thyroid |
| e | `hnlnl` | - | 20 cervical nodal levels |

`fast` is `false` throughout: `disallow_fast` is set upstream on every subtask, and
`total` ran at its native 1.5 mm rather than the 3 mm fast weights.

### Geometry was verified, not assumed

The HaN-Seg DICOM series were converted from these same NRRD files, so the grids should
match exactly. **They do** - checked per case and per organ (`grid_check` and
`gt_grid_matched_ct` in `results.json`): identical size, spacing, origin and direction
cosines (identity, LPS). The nearest-neighbour resample path in `hanseg_eval.py` is
implemented but never fired. Predicted masks return on that same grid, confirmed against
the `X-Shape` / `X-Spacing` / `X-Origin` / `X-Direction` headers.

### Predicted structures with no ground truth here

These produced contours, but **nothing in this report says whether they are correct**:

* `headneck_bones_vessels`: `thyroid_cartilage`, `cricoid_cartilage`, `hyoid`,
  `zygomatic_arch_*`, `styloid_process_*`, `internal_jugular_vein_*`
* `head_glands_cavities`: `eye_lens_*`, `nasopharynx`, `oropharynx`, `hypopharynx`,
  `nasal_cavity_*`, `auditory_canal_*`, `soft_palate`, `hard_palate`

* `total`: `brain`, `vertebrae_C1`-`C7`
* `hnlnl`: **all 20 cervical nodal levels**

This is the uncomfortable part: it includes the internal jugular vein and the laryngeal
cartilages, exactly the structures a head and neck surgeon most wants checked.
RESEARCH.md P1 item 14 already flagged that per-structure Dice for the TotalSegmentator
H&N subtasks (hyoid, cricoid, IJV, constrictors, styloid) was never published. **This run
does not close that gap** - HaN-Seg does not contour them either. It would need SegRap2023
or an in-house contoured set.

## 4. How this compares to published baselines

All figures below are quoted from `RESEARCH.md`; none are invented here.

RESEARCH.md is blunt that **"Dice 0.94" must never be attached to a head and neck
structure** - the TotalSegmentator paper's 0.943 over 104 structures is a
*whole-body* average on a test set deliberately including major pathology, and
RESEARCH.md 5.5 lists quoting it as an H&N claim among the "claims to avoid".
The honest yardsticks it offers instead are the **HaN-Seg challenge winner at DSC
76.9% with HD95 3.5 mm averaged over 30 H&N OARs**, and **SegRap2023 (45 OARs,
400 CTs) at OAR Dice 76.68-86.70%**, with the standing caveat that large OARs are
near clinical grade while small and thin structures remain weak.

Against that 76.9% line, this run splits cleanly into three groups.

**Above the challenge winner, and above the SegRap band.** Mandible 0.932, both
globes 0.912/0.916, thyroid 0.854. Mandible also lands right on the
**DentalSegmentator** figures RESEARCH.md cites (92.2 +/- 6.3% internal, 94.2 +/-
7.4% external, n = 133/123) - reassuring, because that is a purpose-built
mandible model and TotalSegmentator matches it here without one. HD95 of
2.5-2.9 mm on all four is close to the winner's 3.5 mm. The SD on mandible is
0.005 across three patients, i.e. the result is stable, not a lucky case.

**Around or below the line, but explicably.** Submandibular 0.789/0.693 and
z-restricted oesophagus 0.701 sit in the lower half of the SegRap band. Cord at
0.544 z-restricted is below it.

**Well below, and these are the ones that matter clinically.** Parotid 0.483/0.428
and carotid 0.579/0.619. The parotid result is the anomaly: parotid is normally
among the *easier* OARs and sits in the upper half of every published band, yet
here the predicted gland is only **32-35% of the expert volume** - a gross
under-contour, not a boundary disagreement. Carotid is discussed in section 5.

Two methodological cautions RESEARCH.md raises apply directly. First,
**Metrics Reloaded**: Dice alone is misleading for small, tubular and
boundary-critical structures - carotid, canal, airway wall - and NSD/HD95 should
carry the weight. That is exactly why the optic nerve's Dice of 0.433/0.616 is
less informative than its HD95 of 9.3/10.3 mm, which is the number that says the
contour is not usable near the orbital apex. Second, RESEARCH.md recommends
**surface DSC, not volumetric Dice**, as the acceptance metric, because it measures
how much boundary a human must actually redraw. Surface DSC is not computed here;
adding it is the obvious next step before any confidence number reaches the UI.

Finally, **RESEARCH.md P1 item 14** records that per-structure Dice for the
TotalSegmentator H&N subtasks (hyoid, cricoid, IJV, constrictors, styloid) was
never published and that Margin needs its own HaN-Seg/SegRap-based evaluation
before showing any confidence number. This run supplies that for 15 structure
pairs - **but not for any of the five named in that item**, because HaN-Seg does
not contour them either. Item 14 stays open.

For the nodal levels, the paper's own figures are **volumetric Dice 0.86 union /
0.78 per level, surface Dice 0.89 / 0.84** (Putz et al., Front Oncol 2023). None of
that can be checked here - HaN-Seg has no nodal ground truth - and section 5 shows
a failure mode those numbers would not predict on this kind of CT.

## 5. Failure modes

### 5.1 The two carotid classes never meet - the dominant carotid failure

TotalSegmentator splits the carotid across `common_carotid_artery_*` (in `total`)
and `internal_carotid_artery_*` (in `headneck_bones_vessels`). **In all 6 carotids
across the 3 cases there is an unlabelled gap where one stops and the other has
not yet started:**

| case | side | CCA slices | ICA slices | unlabelled gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| case_01 | right | 41-78 | 84-129 | **10 mm** |
| case_01 | left | 24-50 | 92-126 | **82 mm** |
| case_05 | right | 31-63 | 78-112 | **28 mm** |
| case_05 | left | 21-65 | 71-110 | **10 mm** |
| case_12 | right | 25-54 | 64-88 | **18 mm** |
| case_12 | left | 20-53 | 65-88 | **22 mm** |

The gap straddles the **carotid bifurcation** - surgically the most important
centimetre of the vessel, where level II/III nodal disease abuts it and where
resectability is argued. TotalSegmentator has no external carotid class at all, so
the bifurcation is doubly unrepresented. The correlation with the score is exact:
case_01 left has the 82 mm gap and the worst Dice in the run (0.392); case_12
right has an 18 mm gap and the best carotid Dice (0.710). Verified numerically on
case_12 right - at the slice where the expert contour is largest (k = 63, 253
voxels) the prediction is **empty**, and overlap only resumes at k = 64.

This is a structural limitation of the class list, not a quality problem with
either model, and merging the two classes does not fix it: the gap is unlabelled
by both. It is also why the z-restriction barely helps the carotid (0.579 ->
0.596) while it transforms the oesophagus.

### 5.2 HNLNL contours empty air and calls it level II / level V (right)

The nodal sanity check fails identically on all three cases. Levels Ib, III, IVa,
IVb, VIIb and VIII mirror correctly about the midline (|x_L + x_R| = 1-22 mm), and
the II -> III -> IVa -> IVb chain is monotonically superior-to-inferior on both
sides in every case. But **`level_II_right` and `level_V_right` sit at
x = +250 mm** - the patient's *left*, at the edge of the 571 mm field of view,
outside the body:

| case | level | volume | components | centroid x | HU inside |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| case_01 | `level_II_right` | 55.0 mL | 1 | **+248.2 mm** | mean **-1000**, 100% air |
| case_01 | `level_II_left` | 32.4 mL | 1 | +37.1 mm | mean +44.6 (soft tissue) |
| case_01 | `level_V_right` | 159.4 mL | 13 | **+200.3 mm** | mean -845, 84% air |
| case_05 | `level_II_right` | 75.0 mL | 1 | **+251.0 mm** | - |
| case_12 | `level_II_right` | 68.1 mL | 1 | **+249.0 mm** | - |

`level_II_right` is a single solid 55-75 mL blob of **pure air at exactly
-1000 HU**. It is not scattered noise and not a left/right swap: the genuinely
right-sided levels are correctly placed (`level_III_right` at x = -36.9,
`level_Ib_right` at -32.0). `level_V_right` is worse - 160-243 mL against 28-40 mL
for `level_V_left`, in 10-33 disconnected pieces.

Two consequences. First, **HNLNL `_left` does mean patient left**: the six
well-behaved pairs confirm on real data what `backend/ai/README.md` could only
infer from the phantom, so that caveat can be closed. Second, **the right-sided
levels II and V are unusable on this class of CT** and must not be displayed. The
single-component result for `level_II_right` suggests the released
largest-component postprocessing ran and *locked onto the artefact*, which makes
it more dangerous, not less: the output looks clean.

Likely cause, not verified: HaN-Seg CTs are 1024 x 1024 with a wide FOV containing
the immobilisation board and a lot of air, whereas the model was trained on RT
planning CTs; the nnU-Net v1 non-zero crop and the 3.0 x 1.17 x 1.17 mm resample
may be latching onto the field edge. Margin already runs this model single-fold,
3d_fullres only, without mirror TTA and without the author's slice-plane
adjustment - all four documented in `backend/ai/README.md` as making the output
worse than the paper's.

### 5.3 Parotid is under-contoured by two thirds

Predicted parotid volume is 0.35 +/- 0.13 (left) and 0.32 +/- 0.11 (right) of the
expert's, with HD95 22.9/25.0 mm. This is the largest systematic volume error of
any *exact* pair, and it is neither laterality (section 3) nor extent. It needs its
own investigation before parotid contours are shown; the deep lobe and the
retromandibular portion are the first places to look.

### 5.4 Extent mismatches that look like failures but are not

`esophagus` raw Dice 0.123 and `spinal_cord` raw 0.398 are dominated by the AI
labelling far more of the structure than the RT protocol contours. Both recover
substantially when z-restricted (0.701 and 0.544). The raw volume ratios - 10.5x
for oesophagus, 3.8x for cord - are protocol differences, not errors. Reporting
the raw numbers to a clinician would be actively misleading. `larynx_air` vs
`Glottis` + `Larynx_SG` at Dice 0.022 is a **definition mismatch by construction**
(air column vs soft tissue) and is tabulated only to quantify it.

### 5.5 Small neural structures

Optic nerve HD95 is 9.3/10.3 mm against a structure a few millimetres across.
Cochlea, pituitary, chiasm and lacrimal gland have no counterpart at all
(section 2). Margin has nothing validated to offer at the orbital apex or skull
base.

## 6. Overlays

Six axial PNGs in `tools/validation/overlays/`, 22-50 KB each. **Orange fill with a
solid orange edge = AI prediction; cyan outline = HaN-Seg expert.** The slice shown
is the one where the *expert* contour is largest, which is deliberate - it puts the
hardest part of the structure on screen rather than the easiest. Mandible is
rendered in a bone window (WL 400 / WW 1800), carotid in a wide soft-tissue window
(WL 120 / WW 600) so contrast-filled vessels do not saturate to white.

| file | structure | case | Dice |
|---|---|---|---|
| `mandible_best_case_12_dice0.94.png` | mandible | case_12 | 0.935 |
| `mandible_worst_case_01_dice0.93.png` | mandible | case_01 | 0.926 |
| `carotid_right_best_case_12_dice0.71.png` | carotid right | case_12 | 0.710 |
| `carotid_left_best_case_05_dice0.69.png` | carotid left | case_05 | 0.685 |
| `carotid_right_worst_case_05_dice0.54.png` | carotid right | case_05 | 0.535 |
| `carotid_left_worst_case_01_dice0.39.png` | carotid left | case_01 | 0.392 |

**Mandible, best and worst.** The two images are nearly indistinguishable, which is
the point: the spread across three patients is 0.926-0.935. On both, the cyan
expert outline tracks the orange fill so closely that the two read as one contour.
The only visible systematic difference is a hair's breadth of cyan outside the
orange along the outer cortex - the expert includes a thin rim, roughly one voxel,
that the AI stops short of. At 0.5576 mm in-plane that is sub-millimetre, and it is
consistent with MSD 0.40 mm and HD95 2.7 mm. Both slices are through the body and
both rami; the arch, the inferior border and the marrow space are all correct, and
there is no leak into the hyoid, the teeth or the cervical spine. **This is a
contour a surgeon could accept and move on from.**

**Carotid, best cases.** `carotid_left_best_case_05` is the clearest illustration of
the non-structural error: the orange prediction sits squarely inside the cyan
outline, correctly centred on the contrast-filled vessel, but a rim of cyan remains
visible all the way round. The AI is contouring the *lumen* and the expert is
contouring the *vessel*, so the prediction is systematically thin - which is the
volume ratio of 0.66 made visible. `carotid_right_best_case_12` shows the same
vessel correctly identified among several adjacent enhancing structures, with no
confusion with the internal jugular vein.

**Carotid, worst cases - the important ones.** `carotid_left_worst_case_01` shows a
cyan expert contour at the skull base with **no orange anywhere in the frame**. This
is the 82 mm gap from section 5.1: at the slice where the expert contour is largest,
Margin has predicted nothing at all. `carotid_right_worst_case_05` is the same
story one level lower - an expert contour around a grey, poorly-enhancing vessel
near the nasopharynx, and no prediction. These two images are the single most
important thing in this report: **the carotid failure is not a wobbly boundary, it
is absence.** A clinician scrolling through would see a carotid contour appear,
vanish for one to eight centimetres through the bifurcation and skull base, then
reappear - and the missing segment is the part that matters.

honest numbers. A cache hit is 0-20 s throughout, confirming the design assumption
that the *second* look at a series is instant.

**The brief budgeted 4-6 min per task per case. The reality on 1024-matrix data is
5-24 min.** That is why N was cut from 5 to 3: a five-case run would have been
about 4.5 hours of pure inference.

### Where the time goes, and the biggest wins

1. **Downsample before inference - the single biggest lever.** Every one of these
   models resamples internally (TotalSegmentator `total` to 1.5 mm, `hnlnl` to
   3.0 x 1.17 x 1.17 mm). The network never sees 0.56 mm. Handing the runner a
   1024 x 1024 volume means every export, resample, arg-max and write-back moves 4x
   the voxels of a 512 x 512 study for **zero** accuracy benefit at the network.
   Exporting the CT at ~1 mm in plane before inference should cut wall time
   substantially at no cost to the model's input. Caveat: the mask returns on the
   coarser grid and must be resampled back up, which costs precision on thin
   structures (optic nerve, carotid), so make it per-task rather than global.

2. **Share the crop pass across subtasks.** Every head/neck subtask runs `total`
   first to find its crop box. `backend/ai/run_totalseg.py` calls
   `totalsegmentator()` once per task in a plain loop, so **putting several tasks in
   one job does not share it** - three subtasks pay three crop passes. A `total` run
   costs 126-407 s here, so pre-cropping once and feeding the cropped volume to each
   subtask removes two of three crop passes per case. This needs a backend change;
   it is not reachable from the API.

3. **`roi_subset` is already earning its keep - keep it mandatory.** `total` with
   11 ROIs costs 126-407 s; with 2 ROIs, 70-154 s. It is also what holds peak RSS
   near 5.5 GB instead of the ~20 GB an unrestricted 117-class pass wants.

4. **Drop `craniofacial_structures` from any default import set.** At 1020-1706 s it
   is 3-4x any other task and dominates the per-case total, because it segments
   `skull` and `head` across the whole head. If mandible is the only thing wanted
   from it - and mandible is this run's best structure -
   **DentalSegmentator** (CC-BY-4.0, Dice 92.2-94.2%, per RESEARCH.md) is the
   targeted alternative and worth benchmarking against it.

5. **OpenVINO / ONNX export is the only route to seconds.** RESEARCH.md cites
   FLARE 2024 reaching **26 s per case on CPU at Dice 76.8% / NSD 80.5%** under
   strict resource limits, and notes `openvino` and `onnxruntime-openvino` are pip
   wheels with the Intel iGPU usable as an OpenVINO device. No export recipe has
   been published for the TotalSegmentator H&N weights specifically, so this is
   unvalidated work - but it is the only lever with an order of magnitude in it.

### What this means for segment-on-import

A full head and neck set is **43-85 minutes per case** today. Even a good
combination of levers 1-3 plausibly gets 3-5x, which is still ~10 minutes. So
segment-on-import cannot be a blocking step, and should not be presented as one:
kick the job off at import, show the progress bar the AI API already provides, and
let the cache make every subsequent open instant (0-20 s measured). If a
"fast enough to feel live" path is genuinely required, lever 5 is the only
candidate and it needs its own spike.

One operational note worth fixing regardless of speed: on this box completed
**AI jobs disappeared from `GET /api/ai/jobs` mid-run and their `label_id`s then
404'd**, repeatedly, which broke mask retrieval and eventually killed the harness.
The running backend process (started 10:07:59) predates the current
`backend/hnrad/ai.py` (modified 12:46:24) and `--reload` never fired, so the live
server is not necessarily running the retention logic now in source - this may
already be fixed. It reproduced most strongly when a new job was submitted while
another was running, suggesting eviction of a still-running job. `hanseg_eval.py`
works around it by re-submitting (a disk-cache hit) and re-registering labels, and
by never writing scratch files inside the reload-watched tree. Worth confirming
against a freshly started backend before trusting `label_id` lifetime in the
frontend, since CONTRACT.md promises AI labels outlive LRU eviction.

## 8. What to trust, and what not to

**Trust as a starting contour a surgeon corrects:**
mandible (0.932), globes (0.912/0.916), thyroid (0.854). Sub-3 mm HD95, stable
across patients, at or above every published H&N baseline in RESEARCH.md.

**Use with review:** submandibular glands (0.789/0.693).

**Do not trust, and do not display without a warning:**

* **Carotid.** Not because the boundary is soft but because there is a **10-82 mm
  unlabelled gap through the bifurcation** in every case. Anything that measures
  carotid contact, encasement or distance across that gap is measuring a hole.
* **Parotid.** Systematically **one third** of the true gland volume.
* **HNLNL `level_II_right` and `level_V_right`.** 55-243 mL of contoured **air**
  outside the patient, in every case, looking deceptively clean.
* **Raw oesophagus and spinal cord numbers.** Extent mismatches, not errors; quote
  the z-restricted figures or nothing.
* **Optic nerve and anything at the orbital apex or skull base.**

**Not validated at all by this run** (no HaN-Seg ground truth exists): internal
jugular vein, thyroid/cricoid cartilage, hyoid, styloid, zygomatic arch, pharynx
subsites, nasal cavity, palate, teeth, skull, vertebrae C1-C7, brain, and **all 20
nodal levels**. RESEARCH.md P1 item 14 remains open.

### Suggested next steps

1. Add **surface DSC** (RESEARCH.md's recommended acceptance metric) before any
   confidence number is shown in the UI.
2. Extend to **N = 10-15 cases** for usable per-structure confidence intervals;
   this run is N = 3 and the SDs on parotid and carotid are wide.
3. Investigate the **parotid volume deficit** - it is anomalous against every
   published band and may be a fixable preprocessing or window issue.
4. Evaluate **SegRap2023** for the structures HaN-Seg cannot score (IJV,
   cartilages, constrictors), which is the only way to close item 14.
5. Suppress or flag **HNLNL right-sided levels II and V**, and consider an
   inside-body mask as a cheap guard against air contours for every model.

## 10. Reproducing

backend\.venv\Scripts\python.exe tools\validation\hanseg_eval.py --n 3
